## Footnotes

1. Descriptive representation may offer an alternative mechanism to facilitate the legitimacy of discretionary decision (e.g., Meier, 2019 ), as it is presumed that descriptive representation will translate into better administrative decisions ( Webeck and Lee, 2022 ).

2. Jilke and Tummers (2018 : 227) suggest resource deservingness as a third deservingness cue that focuses on efficiently using public resources. Resource-deservingness means that "clients are seen as worthy [...] because they are perceived to be successful in terms of bureaucratic success criteria" ( Jilke and Tummers, 2018 : 231). Since this study is about the prioritization of welfare claimants, we choose to concentrate on the role of clients' need-deservingness and earned deservingness.

3. We carefully constructed the task based on qualitative interviews in street-level offices of the German employment bureaucracy, known as "Job Centers". In these interviews, Job Center employees mentioned almost exactly the setting we describe in our experiment several times as a common example for discretionary decision-making.

4. The norm for non-discriminating behavior is set in the German constitution ( _Grundgesetz_ ) and the Anti-Discrimination Act ( _Allgemeines Gleichbehandlungsgesetz_ , AGG, enacted in 2006). The AGG prohibits any discrimination based on race, ethnic heritage, gender, religion or ideology, physical disability, age, or sexual identity (§ 1 AGG).

5. The sample includes only respondents with German nationality since German nationality is a precondition to entering the civil service. Including respondents with non-German nationality ( _N_ = 88) does not alter any of the substantive results.
