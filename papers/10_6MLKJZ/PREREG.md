## 1. Headline results and targets

Main claim (abstract, p. 32): "While some signs of discrimination exist, need-deservingness is the most important factor shaping respondents' prioritization choices. More importantly, we find no substantial differences in prioritization choices among public employees and citizens, indicating congruence in moral reasoning." The evidence is a paired conjoint experiment (8 tasks, 2 profiles per task) analysed as marginal means (MM) by group and differences in MMs (cregg::cj, estimate "mm" and "mm_diff", respondent-clustered), shown in Figures 1 and 2 (general population, public vs private sector) and Figures 3 and 4 (three student samples).

Plain statement: the paper prints no numeric estimate for its headline result. Every marginal mean, difference and confidence interval appears only as a point in Figures 1-4 and Online Appendix Figures A2-A4, with no number printed on the points (checked on the published PDF and the appendix, 2026-10-10). The only result numbers printed in the Empirical results and Robustness sections are the 0.5 reference value and approximate sample shares ("∼55% vs ∼45%", "∼50%", "∼39%", p. 46). I will not read values off the figures. The numeric targets are therefore the analysis sample sizes in Table 2 (the table that describes the samples behind Figures 1-4), and the headline claim is pre-registered as figure-claim checks FC1 to FC3 with explicit decision rules (below). This is a judgment call for the auditor: with no printed estimates, "reproduced exactly" (class A) cannot be shown on an estimate.

## 3. Robustness checks (pre-registered)

Primary estimate for every check, E1: the Figure 1 marginal mean of Composition = "2 Adults, 3 Childr." for `sector == "Private Sector"` (the largest group, and the need-deservingness level the text singles out: "families with three children have comparably high chances of receiving the advance payment", Deservingness subsection, pp. 44-46). Paper direction and significance: MM above 0.5 (shown in Figure 1; no number printed). "Same direction and significance" for E1 = estimate > 0.5 and 95% interval excludes 0.5. Secondary for every check: the FC1 count (paper: 0) with the same rule as FC1. The primary value goes into results.json `estimate`; the FC1 count goes into its note.

Analysis data for all checks: rows of `pooled_df2` with `sector` in {"Private Sector", "Public Sector"} (the rows the `by = ~ sector` calls use), outcome `selected`, id `respondentIndex`.

R_a. Respondent-level cluster bootstrap. `set.seed(20261009)` once; B = 1000 resamples; within each sector draw respondents (`respondentIndex`) with replacement, same number as in the original data, keep all 16 rows of each drawn respondent (give duplicates new ids). In each resample compute MMs as the unweighted mean of `selected` by feature level and sector (this equals cregg's unweighted MM), and mm_diff = Public minus Private for every level. Report: original-sample E1 (from cregg), bootstrap percentile 95% CI (2.5th and 97.5th percentiles), bootstrap SE; p-value = two-sided bootstrap share, min(1, 2 * min(share of E1* <= 0.5, share >= 0.5)). Secondary: number of FC1 levels whose percentile CI for mm_diff excludes 0. Same direction and significance as defined above.

R_b. Outlier exclusion. The outcome is binary and all predictors are randomized categorical attributes, so neither |z| > 3 on the outcome nor a continuous main predictor exists. Pre-set substitute: top/bottom 1% of respondent survey duration, the Qualtrics column `Duration (in seconds)` in each raw file (`data_rh.csv`, `data_UHH.csv`, `data_HSVN.csv`, `data_civic.csv`; ignore the two Qualtrics header rows, whose Duration is not numeric). Join to `pooled_df2` by `ResponseId` = `respondentIndex`; within each of the four samples, among respondents present in `pooled_df2`, compute the 1st and 99th percentiles (`quantile(type = 7)`) and drop respondents strictly below the 1st or strictly above the 99th. Rerun the Figure 1 and Figure 2 `cj()` calls exactly as in 04_analysis.R on the reduced data. Report E1 with cregg CI and the z-test p-value against 0.5 (`(estimate - 0.5) / std.error`), the FC1 count, and respondents dropped per sample. Same direction and significance as defined above.

R_c. Alternative estimand: AMCEs instead of marginal means. Run `cj(pooled_df2_sector, selected ~ Gender + Age + Citizenship + Composition + Unemployment + Supportive + Since + Education, estimate = "amce", by = ~ sector, id = ~ respondentIndex)` (cregg default baselines = first factor level, i.e. "1 Adult" for Composition). Primary: AMCE of Composition = "2 Adults, 3 Childr." vs "1 Adult" for Private Sector, with clustered SE and p-value; same direction and significance = AMCE > 0 and p < .05. Secondary (replaces the FC1 count for this check): omnibus test of sector differences, `cregg::cj_anova(pooled_df2_sector, selected ~ <same 8 features>, by = ~ sector)`; same conclusion as the paper (no difference) if p >= .05; report the F statistic and p.
